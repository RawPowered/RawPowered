# Why this portfolio is new

**The public GitHub portfolio is new. The work behind it is not.**

For roughly 15 years, I worked inside operating companies and alongside their leaders, often with broad, independent responsibility for the systems supporting sales, service, fulfillment, and growth. My focus was improving the operation—not turning company work into a personal showcase. Code, CRM data, workflows, customer records, and operating details belonged to each business and were often private by design. I did not keep a parallel public archive.

That history is harder to demonstrate now. Some companies closed, platforms aged, systems were migrated, and the records that remain are incomplete. The repositories here are public-safe demonstrations and carefully scoped case studies, created so people can review my decisions, interfaces, integrations, and automation. I label historical examples when the record is partial, distinguish reported outcomes from evidence that can still be checked, and keep private company and customer information out. Fictional-data demos are identified as such.

## How I work

I have usually worked as a hands-on, entrepreneurial operator, close to the people responsible for the business. I learn the operation, find the constraint, then design, repair, integrate, or automate the system and carry the work through delivery. That direct context has often let me move quickly without losing sight of the people who depend on the result.

Much of that work predates widely available commercial LLM assistants. I use AI tools in development today, while retaining responsibility for requirements, architecture, source review, debugging, and release scope.

## Where I want to contribute

My ambition is to contribute to frontier work in space, AI, and exploration. I want to help wherever my experience is a strong fit, take on difficult problems, and grow through the work. I am also open to impactful roles across enterprise technology, construction, energy and solar, CRM platforms, and operations systems. The common thread is meaningful responsibility: understand what matters, build something useful, and improve the outcome.

This page explains why the public artifacts begin recently, while my work in technology began much earlier.
